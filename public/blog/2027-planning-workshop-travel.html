Get a head start on your 2027 team planning workshops

September 2026 · 5 min read

The best 2027 planning workshops are half-booked before most teams have finished their December wind-down. Not because those planners are superhuman — because they started the travel while there was still slack in the calendar, the roster, and the budget.

A team planning workshop only works if the whole team is in the room. That sounds obvious, and it’s exactly the part that goes wrong. Someone’s flight lands during the first session. A room never got added to the block. A director you needed for the goals conversation is stuck at an airport an hour away. The agenda was fine; the logistics quietly undid it.

Why early beats the January rush

Wait until the new year and you’re bidding against everyone else who waited. Airfares for late-January and February travel climb as the dates approach, hotels near anywhere worth meeting sell out their meeting space first, and the transfer companies that handle group pickups get booked solid. Start in September or October and you have real choice: better fares, a proper room block held at a rate you negotiated, and transfers you actually confirmed instead of hoped for.

Early booking also buys you the one thing the January rush never gives you — time to fix mistakes. A flight booked in the wrong city is a five-minute change three months out. It’s a change fee and a scramble three days out.

Plan around a roster that keeps moving

Here’s the tension nobody warns you about: you need to book offsite travel early, but your attendee list won’t hold still. People get hired. People leave. A skip-level decides to join. Someone’s partner has surgery that week and they drop out. If you lock everything in September and never revisit it, you end up with rooms for people who aren’t coming and no seat for the two who joined in November.

So treat the roster as a living thing. Book the core group early to capture the rates, then re-check the list against your bookings every few weeks as the team firms up. The goal isn’t a perfect list in September. It’s a list you keep honest all the way to the workshop.

Keep four files telling the same story

By the time a corporate event travel plan is real, the details live in separate places: the attendee list in one export, flights in an airline or agency file, the hotel’s rooming list, and the ground-transfer schedule. Each one is right on its own. The problem is the gaps between them — the registered attendee with no flight, the room in the block with no name attached, the arrival that lands after the transfer already left.

That’s where cross-checking earns its keep. Instead of reading each file separately and trusting your eye to catch an absence, you compare them and let the mismatches surface. Every gap gets flagged while there’s still time to clear it with a short email — not discovered at the front desk the night before.

What to lock before the holidays

A short list keeps the whole thing from sliding into January: confirm the dates and venue, hold the room block, book flights for the people you know are coming, and line up transfers against the actual arrival times. Then set a recurring cross-check — attendee list against flights, hotel, and car — so every change to the roster gets caught within a week. Do that, and the workshop starts on time with everyone present, and the first session is about 2027, not about who’s missing.
