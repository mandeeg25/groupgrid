Case closed: playing detective with your event travel data

August 2026 · 4 min read

Every event planner is, quietly, a detective. The case is always the same: a hundred people are supposed to arrive, sleep, and get to the venue on time, and somewhere in the paperwork, a few of them won’t. The clues are all there. The trick is knowing how to read them before the trail goes cold at the front desk.

The scene: four spreadsheets, one truth

Walk into the crime scene and you’ll find it exactly as every planner leaves it. Registration in one export. The flight manifest in another. A rooming list in a third. Car transfers in a fourth. Each file swears it’s telling the truth, and each one is, but only about its own corner of the story. The full picture, the one where every attendee actually gets where they need to be, lives in the gaps between the files. That’s where you start looking.

The usual suspects

Work enough events and you learn the repeat offenders by name. They show up in the attendee travel data again and again:

  • The no-show booking. A name on the flight manifest who never appears on the registration list, a ghost you’re paying for.
  • The stranded guest. Registered, confirmed, expected, but no room and no flight to their name.
  • The date that doesn’t agree. A flight landing the day after check-in, or a room booked for a night nobody’s staying.
  • The wrong airport. A transfer waiting at one terminal while the guest lands at another across town.

None of them look guilty on their own. Read any single file and everything checks out. It’s only when you put the lists side by side that the alibis fall apart.

Follow the mismatches

Good detectives don’t reread the same statement hoping something jumps out. They cross-reference. A travel booking cross-check works the same way: instead of scanning each list on its own, you hold them up against one another and let the contradictions surface. Who registered but has no flight? Who has a hotel room but never registered? Whose dates don’t line up across files?

This is the method that actually helps you find travel booking issues, because the thing you’re hunting is an absence, a missing row, a name that should match and doesn’t. Eyeballing rows is exactly how a mind glides past a gap. Cross-checking is what forces the gap into the light and turns it into a lead you can follow.

Case closed

Every mismatch you catch weeks out is a case closed on paper: a two-line email to fix a date, a room re-booked, a ghost passenger struck from the bill. Every one you miss becomes a scramble at check-in, when the trail is cold and the fees are steep. The difference isn’t how carefully you read. It’s whether you cross-checked at all.

That’s the whole case: gather the files, compare them against each other, and let every flagged mismatch tell you where to look next. Do the detective work early, and there’s no mystery left waiting at the door.
