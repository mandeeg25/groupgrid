Preventing travel gaps before your event

August 2026 · 5 min read

The worst place to discover a travel problem is the hotel front desk on arrival day. By then, the fix is expensive, stressful, and visible to your attendee. Almost every one of those surprises traces back to a mismatch between what someone registered for and what actually got booked — and every mismatch is catchable weeks earlier.

Here are the five gaps that cause the most check-in chaos, and how to find each one before it becomes a problem.

1. Registered, but no flight booked

An attendee completes registration and expects travel, but they never appear on the flight manifest. It’s easy to miss because their name is on your registration list — the absence is what you have to notice, and absences are exactly what the eye skips over.

Catch it by matching every registered attendee who requested a flight against the manifest, and flagging anyone with no matching record. A quick confirmation email weeks out is far cheaper than a last-minute fare.

2. Registered, but no hotel booked

The same problem on the lodging side: a confirmed attendee with no room on the rooming list. This one bites hardest during peak season, when the block sells out and there’s no room left to add.

Catch it by cross-referencing registration against the hotel roster and surfacing anyone who needs a room but doesn’t have one — while the block still has availability.

3. Travel booked for someone who never registered

The mirror image, and the one that quietly wastes budget: a flight or a hotel room reserved for a name that isn’t on your registration list. Sometimes it’s a cancellation that never got released; sometimes it’s a duplicate or a guest who dropped.

Catch it by flagging every travel record with no matching registration, so you can confirm or release it before you pay for a seat nobody uses.

4. Dates that don’t line up

The attendee is registered, has a flight, and has a room — but the numbers don’t agree. A flight that lands the day after hotel check-in. A departure booked before check-out. A car transfer scheduled for the wrong day. Each one means a stranded guest or a wasted night.

Catch it by comparing arrival and departure dates across the flight, hotel, and transfer records for each person, and flagging any that disagree by even a day.

5. The wrong airport or dates outside the window

An attendee books into the wrong airport, or travels outside the approved event window. Neither is necessarily an error — but each is worth a quick confirmation so a genuine mistake doesn’t turn into a missed session or a transfer that can’t reach them.

Catch it by checking arrival airports against your preferred list and travel dates against your event window, then confirming the exceptions.

Make the check routine, not heroic

None of these gaps are hard to fix once you can see them — the challenge is seeing them at all across four or five separate spreadsheets. The planners who avoid check-in surprises aren’t more careful; they just reconcile early and systematically, turning a stack of files into one clear list of who still needs attention.
